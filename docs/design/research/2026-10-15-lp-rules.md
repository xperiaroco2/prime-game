## 1. The game in one paragraph

LP is a first-person social deduction game by Mirage Creative Lab, a team of three siblings in France ([team](https://miragecreativelab.com/the-team/) [S]). It runs on Unreal Engine 5.5 (PN 0.4.0 [V]).

- **Release:** early access 2024-07-22, 1.0 on 2025-11-18 [V] ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2780980/LOCKDOWN_Protocol/), PN 1.0.0).
- **Players:** "1 to 16" on the store [V].
- **Reviews today:** 82% positive of 9,504 English reviews; 75% of the last 30 days' 134 [V] ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2780980/LOCKDOWN_Protocol/), fetched today).
- **Store tags:** one of them is "Perma Death" [V] (same page).

**The setup:** employees do a shared task list against a clock; a few dissidents try to stop them "without getting caught" [V] ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2780980/LOCKDOWN_Protocol/)).

**The roadmap:** the team said LP is "mostly in a finished state". They promise no new maps and no official mod support [V] (What's next post, 2025-11-18). Since 1.6.1 there is a "modded lobby" flag [V] (PN 1.6.1).

## 2. Session and round structure

### 2.1 Phases
- **Lobby:** a walkable lobby area with wall panels, video tutorials on a big screen, a museum (from 1.0) and a mannequin for customization [V] (PN 0.1.16, 0.14.0, 1.0.0).
- **No damage in the lobby:** since 0.12.0 nobody can deal damage there and items cannot exist there, "to prevent cheaters from killing the host attempting to ban them" [V] (PN 0.12.0).
- **Settings:** the host sets the session rules. Presets can be saved (PN 1.2.0). There are difficulty presets: Training ("learn the tasks"), Beginner ("a simplified version"), Normal and Custom [V] (PN 0.1.26).
- **Spawn:** players spawn randomly "to avoid unfair advantage" (PN 0.1.13). Since 0.14.0 the host picks a grouped spawn around the central tree (the default) or a sparse spawn across the map [V] (PN 0.14.0).
- **Role reveal:** no source describes the reveal screen. One player-written guide says the music differs between employee and dissident [P] ([facts guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3609667203)).
- **Round:** real-time, continuous, no pauses (§5). A clock sits in the top-left corner and makes "a very quiet sound every second" [P] (same guide).
- **Mid-round joins:** players can join mid-round. 1.3.0 fixed "players joining mid round no longer spawn dead in the garden" [V] (PN 1.3.0). [A] This suggests a mid-round joiner normally enters alive or as a ghost; I could not confirm which.
- **End:** I found no source that describes the end screen.

### 2.2 Length
- **Default round:** 8 minutes. The developers wrote that the next map would get "longer rounds to introduce mechanics that we couldn't fit in the current 8 minute round" [V] (Dev update #2).
- **Host setting:** a player confirms "a standard game has an 8 minute time limit … as much as 20 minutes" [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4635988522932715980/)). The maximum was raised "from 20min to 60min" [V] (PN 0.12.1).
- **Short by design:** the game is "designed to play in short sessions like most party games" [V] ([Dev update #1](https://miragecreativelab.com/bonjour-tout-le-monde/)). NamuWiki says a round ends "in less than 10 minutes" [V] ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/LOCKDOWN%20Protocol)).

### 2.3 Map and player count
- **One map**, balanced on purpose: "the current map is balanced for 5-8 players and the size is fine tuned around that. Making the map bigger would make the experience way too uneven" [V] (Dev update #2).
- **The cap:** 8 until 0.13.0. Then 16, but only with "duplicate player colors" allowed, set before the lobby is created [V] (PN 0.13.0). [A] They raised the cap without resizing the map, so 16 is a chaos mode, not the tuned game.
- **Sectors:** the map has coloured sectors: Botanic (green), Medical (light blue), Storage (orange), Pizzushi restaurant (grey), Mechanical (blue), Security (red), Office (yellow). The locked rooms are optional extras [V] ([wiki Map](https://lockdown-protocol-game.fandom.com/wiki/Map)). Decorative plants match each sector's colour, to make the sectors readable [V] (PN 0.2.0).

## 3. Roles and counts

- **Two roles only:** employee and dissident [V] ([store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2780980/LOCKDOWN_Protocol/), [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/LOCKDOWN%20Protocol)). No neutral or special roles exist in any patch note [V] (all PNs).
- **Dissidents are mechanically identical to employees:** they play "with no unique role specific game mechanics", apart from the ability to recognize each other [V] ([developer site](https://miragecreativelab.com/lockdown-protocol/)). NamuWiki adds that the dissident "displays a sign showing who is on the same side" and "has no additional abilities except for being able to identify enemies" [V] ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/LOCKDOWN%20Protocol)). I could not find what the sign looks like (a mark on the visor, an outline or a name).
- **Count:** set by the host; I found no automatic count per player count.
  - Players report 2 dissidents in 8-player lobbies as the norm: "With 2 dissidents and 8 players there is a 25% chance" [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4764333414208635266/)), and "most of the time its still a 2 vs 6" [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/603020257754390789/)).
  - "2 diss + default setting is good for a full lobby" [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4428814237729244751/)).
  - A player asks whether the setting is "a specific value or set to 0-2", which suggests a range option [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4635988976587584829/)).
  - 0 dissidents is allowed. Players then suspect and kill each other anyway: "True social experiment mode" [V] ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/LOCKDOWN%20Protocol)).
- **The pick is a pure random draw.** Players complain the same people are dealt dissident repeatedly, and that there is no "you just had it" protection [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4764333414208635266/)).

## 4. Win and loss

- **Employees** have exactly one win: finish every task before the timer ends. "Unlike other mafia games, killing all the DISSIDENTs does not automatically result in victory" [V] ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/LOCKDOWN%20Protocol)). A player says the same: dissidents win "when the times runs out with at least one task still being open" [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4635988779091438487/)).
- **Dissidents** win by killing every employee, or when time runs out with a task open. "Anything other than clearing a mission is a DISSIDENT victory" [V] ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/LOCKDOWN%20Protocol)).
- **No other win or loss** appears in any patch note [V].
- [A] This is almost exactly our base mode's win table (GDD §3 and §10). The difference: in LP death is permanent, so "kill every employee" is a real dissident win. Ours needs every engineer to have left the match.

## 5. Meetings and votes: none

- **No meetings, no voting.** "This game does not provide time for separate meetings and voting. Therefore … EMPLOYEE in the citizen role has no restrictions on killing others. For this reason, it is common to see cases where members of the same EMPLOYEE become suspicious of each other and kill each other" [V] ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/LOCKDOWN%20Protocol)).
- **Justice is violence:** anyone may attack anyone at any time.
- **No pause:** a player compares it badly with Among Us, where the crew can "pause a round and vote" [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/599650454957209860/)).
- **Kicks stand in for votes:** after killing a real dissident, players get kicked "because others assume they are killing at random" [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4428814417438297109/)).

## 6. Information each side gets

**Employees know:**
- The shared task list and the procedures, on the tablet (key Q). The tablet carries "a map, task procedures, and general game information" [V] ([developer site](https://miragecreativelab.com/lockdown-protocol/)), and "the tablet (Q) shows tasks" [P] ([beginner guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3550085527)).
- From 1.0 the tablet has shortcuts and basics tutorials [V] (PN 1.0.0). Video tutorials per task came in 0.1.16 [V].

**Dissidents know** all of the above, plus who the other dissidents are [V] ([developer site](https://miragecreativelab.com/lockdown-protocol/)). Nothing else.

**Identity cues, deliberately limited:**
- One colour per player since 0.1.31, so "shout the attacker's colour" is the core callout [V] (PN 0.1.31); [P] ([beginner guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3550085527)).
- Nicknames above heads were considered and rejected: they "cause serious balance issues to identify players" [V] ([Dev update #1](https://miragecreativelab.com/bonjour-tout-le-monde/)).
- Players open and close each eye with keys (wink), and "people see your eyes, so you can communicate with them" [V] (PN 0.6.0). Winking while dead was removed because "you could communicate while dead with that" [V] (PN 0.6.1).
- Hands-up is a gesture. It drops the held item, first as a throw and since 1.6.1 as a true drop [V] (PN 0.1.25, 1.6.1). [A] It is the "I am harmless" signal.
- Holding the secondary action shows an item's exact stats [V] (PN 0.1.13). The numbers are public knowledge.

**Information tools:**
- **Cameras:** a camera room has screens showing the map's cameras [V] (PN 0.6.3, 1.4.0). A player guide says cameras help find weapons and access cards, that "dissidents often … destroy them", and that the offices have none [P] ([beginner guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3550085527)). Players also say nobody uses cameras to find dissidents [P] ([thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/599654119441961615/)).
- **Weapon crates as an information event:** an opened crate shows its content "in shaped foam that lets the other players know what was inside" [V] (PN 0.2.0). Everyone can read which gun is now loose.
- **Sound:** voice runs through a custom propagation system that sends sound along paths through the level instead of muffling it through walls [V] ([developer site](https://miragecreativelab.com/lockdown-protocol/)). Sprinting and jumping make noise [V] (same). Item hits, shots and breaking items were made about 50% louder in range [V] (PN 1.3.0). A long-standing bug made some microphones global instead of local [V] (PN 1.4.0).

## 7. The body: health, stamina, movement

- **Stamina is the core rule** [V] ([developer site](https://miragecreativelab.com/lockdown-protocol/)):
  - Sprinting and jumping cost stamina.
  - Stamina absorbs damage before health.
  - With no stamina left, actions cost health instead.
  - Stamina sits inside the health bar, so lower health means a smaller stamina pool.
  - NamuWiki adds: "If you attempt to perform additional actions while your stamina is depleted, your stamina will decrease and you may die" [V].
  - Since 0.14.2, "self-inflicted death (from stamina or fall)" is named in the notes [V] (PN 0.14.2). You can kill yourself by over-exerting.
- **Regeneration:** stamina "now recharge[s] much faster when closer to full … to better reward the stamina management" [V] (PN 0.1.17).
- **Movement:** walk, jog, sprint, jump [V] ([developer site](https://miragecreativelab.com/lockdown-protocol/)). Forward sprint went from 7 to 8 m/s [V] (PN 0.1.24). Stepping over small obstacles and landing from a fall slow you more since 0.14.0 [V] (PN 0.14.0).
- **Falls:** "Jumping down kills you instantly" from certain heights [P] ([facts guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3609667203)).
- **Health numbers:** no official figure. [A] The patch notes imply 100 health and 100 stamina: a 40-damage frontal knife hit "now requires 3 frontal hits to kill full stamina players", and a fish's 50 stamina damage needs "2 hits to disarm" (PN 0.7.0 [V]).
- **Disarm:** [A] emptying a target's stamina seems to knock the held item out of its hands; the fish "always disarms" [P] ([Hardcoregamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/lockdown-protocol-best-weapons/)).
- **Healing:** almost none. Cookies (Christmas theme, from 1.3.0) heal "on frontal hit". Popsicles (summer theme, 1.6.0) heal "by a small amount". Their healing was doubled in 1.6.0 [V] (PN 1.3.0, 1.6.0). The wiki lists the cookie at −10 frontal health damage [V] ([wiki Cookie](https://lockdown-protocol-game.fandom.com/wiki/Cookie)); a player guide says 20 HP [P] ([beginner guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3550085527)).
- **Unshipped heals:** a healing syringe and a stamina syringe were planned, "crafted from plant samples", but never shipped [V] ([Dev update #1](https://miragecreativelab.com/bonjour-tout-le-monde/), Dev update #2). "The game files contain grenades" [P] ([facts guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3609667203)).

## 8. Combat

### 8.1 The damage model
- **Three numbers per hit:** health damage, stamina damage and slow or knockback.
- **Back hits differ:** melee has separate numbers for hits from behind [V] ([wiki Items](https://lockdown-protocol-game.fandom.com/wiki/Items)).
- **Every attack costs the attacker stamina**, gunshots included [V] (wiki; PN 0.2.2 "Weapons shot stamina cost increased"; PN 1.4.3 "Increased stamina cost per shot of the SMG").
- **Design intent (0.15.0):** "bring more opportunity for defense and escape. The goal is to leave more space for the social aspect, while still rewarding good execution of assassination plans." They raised melee stamina costs "to further punish missed attacks and reward self control". They raised knockback "to make escape out of a duel much easier". They reduced frontal damage "to promote and reward planned assassination" [V] (PN 0.15.0, 0.14.4).

Rules around melee:
- Knife spawns are a host setting, "from none to 2 knives" [V] (PN 0.12.0). In 2024 one knife spawned in about 84% of 25 observed games [P] ([item guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3329624482)).
- A back attack with a fish sticks it on the target's back (an April-fools joke they kept) [V] (PN 0.6.2).
- Mugs, cookies, candy canes and fish break on impact [V] (PN 1.3.0, [wiki Fish](https://lockdown-protocol-game.fandom.com/wiki/Fish)).

Notes on the guns:
- **Pellets:** the shorty fires 24 pellets and the shotgun 13 [P] ([facts guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3609667203)). That makes the shorty a close-range one-shot [P] ([item guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3329624482)).
- **Shorty nerf:** 0.15.0 "reduced body damage and increased headshot damage and knockback of the Shorty … reduce the ease of oneshot" [V].
- **Noise:** the pistol is near-silent and the revolver very loud [P] ([UA guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3321306009)). Gun sounds were "rebalanced across all range for gameplay purpose" [V] (PN 0.1.13).
- **Accuracy:** the revolver and pistol got harder sustained long shots and a more accurate first shot [V] (PN 0.15.0). The rifle's accuracy went up and the SMG's down [V] (PN 1.4.0).
- **Ammo is random:** field guns "can be empty and have generally less ammo"; crate guns "always have ammo, but are rarely full" [V] (PN 0.2.2).
- **Heavy weapons cannot go in the bag**, so a rifle carrier is visible [V] (PN 0.15.0).

### 8.4 Where guns come from, as changed patch by patch
This is the most-iterated system in LP. [A] The history is a lesson in itself.

1. **Launch (July 2024):** guns lay on the field. The "run to get a gun asap" meta "has been a problem since the beginning" [V] (PN 0.2.0). Guns now "spawn evenly across all sectors" [V] (PN 0.1.22).
2. **0.2.0, the weapon crates:** guns spawn only in crates at fixed places, opened by "cracking a code" [V].
   - The code is 4 digits with no repeats. Feedback per digit is grey (absent), yellow (wrong place) or blue (right place) [P] ([gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/lockdown-protocol-weapon-cases-locations-guide.html)).
   - There are 7 cases, one per sector [P] (same).
3. **0.2.2, the crates went down badly** ("haven't been received well", "rushed"), and they ran a survey [V]:
   - 50% of guns in crates and 50% on the field.
   - Crates start locked and "randomly unlock during the session after a few minutes".
   - A failed code locks the crate for a random time.
   - Attempts went from 9 to 7.
   - Guns became "overall less lethal".
4. **0.3.0:** only 3 active crates, one per weapon. "For each empty crate, there will be one weapon on the field", with a shorter unlock delay, "to reduce the attention and time spent opening crates" [V].
5. **0.11.0:** empty decoy crates became "moderately rare" [V].
6. **0.12.0:** a host setting for gun spawns: none, default, crates only, field only [V].
7. **0.15.0, the heavy crate:** a heavy-weapon crate in the armory needs a second access card. It "will always contain an empty weapon, that needs to be charged". It spawned 50% of the time; from 1.2.0 it is always there [V] (PN 0.15.0, 1.2.0). Since 0.15.3 the rifle spawns full, with an ammo setting [V].
8. **Pool:** the shotgun was added in 1.1.0 and the SMG in 1.2.0, with a setting choosing which heavy guns can spawn [V].

### 8.5 Reloading
- **The reload station** is in the armory: "recharge any weapon, by placing the matching plant sample" [V] (PN 0.11.0). Since 0.12.0 "each refill now grants only half the ammo" [V].
- **Plants come from the Analysis task's Botanic area**, so reloading costs task-economy time [A]. The plant mapping is in the table in §8.3 ([wiki Armory](https://lockdown-protocol-game.fandom.com/wiki/Armory) [V/P]).
- **Host setting:** the station can be disabled, random or enabled [V] (PN 0.13.0).

### 8.6 Explosives
- **C4 and detonator.** C4 alone "does nothing without the Detonator". The detonator fires it "from any distance" and the blast deals "massive damage" [P] ([item guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3329624482)).
- **Detonator:** it works from the inventory; the blast kills near the centre; it spawns about 25% of the time [P] ([Hardcoregamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/lockdown-protocol-best-weapons/)).
- **Setting:** detonator spawn chance runs "from none to guaranteed" [V] (PN 0.12.0). The C4 got a visual revamp and a blast effect [V] (PN 0.8.0).
- **The developers' framing:** C4 is the model of "unique items that have a very specific but situational use and can bring a fun twist" [V] (Dev update #2).

## 9. Items and inventory

- **Inventory:** with free hands you point, interact or pick up. A held item can be inspected, dropped, thrown, consumed or swung "using only two buttons". "Most items" fit one pocket slot, for "a total of two items" [V] ([developer site](https://miragecreativelab.com/lockdown-protocol/)).
- **Heavy items cannot be pocketed** [P] ([Hardcoregamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/lockdown-protocol-best-weapons/)), and neither can the fish [P] ([item guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3329624482)). Heavy guns cannot go in the bag [V] (PN 0.15.0).
- **Throwing:** a "snap throw" lands an item in a machine slot "for super cool skill shots" [V] (PN 0.6.0).
- **On death the bag drops** (fixed in 0.14.4) [V]. A held item "flies farther" than a pocketed one [P] ([facts guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3609667203)).
- **Hiding spots are a designed feature.** The developers curated places where items can be hidden: "Rebalanced place where you could hide items" (PN 0.4.0). In 0.5.0 they removed collisions inside the pressure machines and under tables, and added props "to better visually reflect the collisions" [V] (PN 0.5.0). They walled off unreachable spots, such as the restaurant gap "that allowed items in unreachable areas" [V] (PN 1.5.0).
- **Item families:**
  - **Task items:** batteries, fuses, screwdrivers, containers, packages, gas bottles, vent filters, samples, rice, fish [V] ([wiki](https://lockdown-protocol-game.fandom.com/api.php?action=query&list=allpages&aplimit=500&format=json)). Task items scale with which tasks are on, for example "Packages: 100% if DELIVERY is allocated" [P] ([item guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3329624482)).
  - **Weapons:** §8.
  - **"Miscellaneous" junk per room:** machine junk, mugs, pencils, measuring tape, tape roll, food cans, medication. These "have no task purpose but can be used to defend yourself" and spawn "organically in fitting areas" [V] (PN 0.8.0, 0.11.0).
  - **Fun items:** cassettes for music players [V] (PN 0.1.13); a cassette and boombox lure is a player trick [P] ([VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/lockdown-protocol-is-the-newest-game-my-friend-group-is-obsessed-with-and-for-good-reason/)). Also rare easter eggs that unlock charms (PN 0.7.0, 0.8.0), and seasonal healing snacks (§7).

## 10. The defibrillator, revives and the locked medical rooms

**0.7.0 rules** [V] (PN 0.7.0):
- The defibrillator always spawns on its wall slot in the medical area.
- It can be picked up at any time but must be charged first.
- The wall slot has two battery slots that must be filled at the same time to charge it.
- Once charged, it has one charge for a "shocking pulse" that "will either resurrect a dead player, or deal heavy damage on a living one!"

**Problems at launch:**
- The release was reverted the same day for game-breaking bugs (REVERT TO 0.6.3) [V].
- After the re-release, revived players could see and talk to ghosts: "living people can see and speak to ghosts now" [P] ([0.7.0 comments](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/eventcomments/599650454957213823/)). It was fixed in 0.7.1 [V].
- Defibrillators duplicated when the carrier took damage at the dock [P] ([search](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/search/?q=defibrillator&gidforum=4848240281337283428&include_deleted=1)).

**More defibrillators:**

## 17. What makes it work [A]

1. **One rule set for everyone.** Same body, same items, same kill power. A dissident is only a person who knows a secret and has a different goal. Every threat is readable and every action deniable.
2. **Stamina is a single resource for running, fighting and soaking hits**, with exhaustion able to kill you. Every chase and every swing is a decision. A missed swing is costly by design (PN 0.15.0).
3. **Back hits beat frontal ones** (knife 70 vs 40). This rewards planning, positioning and "don't turn your back on anyone". It makes trust a physical act: walking ahead of someone.
4. **Tasks, items and combat share one economy.** Batteries charge the defibrillator, plants reload guns, the alarm uses a fuse and the heavy crate uses a card. One object pulls in two directions.
5. **Scarcity with choice:** one or two cards and three rooms, "choose wisely".
6. **Public information events:** the crate's foam shows which gun left; the camera room; colours; winks; hands-up. Most information is physical and social, not UI.
7. **Short rounds** (8 min) on a map tuned for 5 to 8 players.
8. **The host decides the balance.** Almost every disputed rule is a toggle, so friend groups tune their own game (presets, Training mode).
9. **Comedy props:** fish slapped on backs, cookies that heal in the face, water-gun skins, snap throws into machine slots.

## 19. Mechanic → lesson → an original direction for our alien party

The third column is **a proposal for the engineer**, not a decision. Every row must fit our pillars: roles mechanically identical, knockdown and respawn instead of perma-death, macro over micro skill, open knowledge, comic violence.

| Mechanic in LP | What it teaches us | Proposal for the engineer (our own idea) |
|---|---|---|
| Stamina is the run, attack and shield pool; exhaustion can kill (§7) | One resource makes every chase a decision without aim skill | A "party buzz" meter that pays for sprinting and dancing and soaks the first hits; run it dry and you trip into the knockdown, not death. Refilled only at snack spots, so feeding yourself is a choice |
| Back hits do 70, front 40 (§8.2) | Positioning beats aim; trust is physical | A prank from behind: party props (whoopee cushion, confetti popper) knock down only from behind and just stagger from the front. Comic, aim-free, readable |
| Any item is a weapon, with public stats (§8.2, §9) | Weapons that are also task items give alibis ("I was carrying it for the task") | Every party prop has a task use and a slapstick use. An "inspect" card shows both to everyone (open knowledge) |
| Gun rush, then crates with a code (§8.4) | Free lethal weapons wreck the social side; LP needed five patches | No guns. A rare "gift box" opened by a short shared glyph puzzle everyone can see; the lid shows the silhouette of what came out |
| Crate foam tells everyone what left (§6) | A physical information event is better than a HUD line | When the box opens, the whole party hears a themed jingle that names the item type. Everyone knows a dangerous prop is loose, nobody knows who has it |
| Reload with plants from a task area (§8.5) | Combat stays tied to the task economy | Rare props recharge at a task station with an ingredient (the popper refills at the drinks machine with one coloured drink). Using it costs your side task time |
| One card, three rooms, "choose wisely" (§14) | Scarce access creates strategy and arguments | One "VIP wristband" per match opens one of three party rooms, each with a different perk (a shortcut, spare task supplies, a rare prop). Who uses it, and on which room, is a team argument |
| Alarm with a fuse; pulling the fuse kills it forever (§13) | A public alarm the attacker can disable gives both sides a play | A party horn per area that blasts a "come here!" sound everyone hears. A human can unplug it for the rest of the match, but unplugging is loud and seen on the room's light |
| Defibrillator: two batteries, revive or a heavy hit (§10) | Revives mixed with damage became a weapon and a deduction-breaker | Our knockdown already lets anyone raise a player. A shared "early wake-up" station that cuts a teammate's 30 s wait, paid with task items, with no damage use |
| Ghosts invisible and talking only to ghosts; wink removed when dead (§11) | LP had to patch every leak from the dead to the living | We already chose "dead hear no voice" and respawn. Keep the dead busy instead: picking their capsule and perk while watching, not chatting |
| Hands-up drops your item; wink; one colour each (§6) | Cheap body language carries the social game in voice-first play | Alien gestures with meaning: a "show empty hands" emote that drops the item, an antenna wiggle as a silent signal. Unique colours stay the callout |
| Healing only through rare snacks hit into the face (§7) | Healing is a comedy moment, not a menu | "Feed a friend": throw a snack into a friend's face to heal them. A human can feed you a spicy one that staggers you. Same item, two outcomes |
| Pressure machine explodes and hurts everyone nearby (§16) | A room-wide failure is a memorable shared story | A task whose failure knocks down everyone in the room (the overloaded fondue fountain, the fireworks crate). Humans may provoke it on purpose, and everyone sees it coming |
| Host toggles for everything; Training and Beginner presets (§15) | Friend groups tune the game; new players need a gentler mode | Presets for "first party", "normal" and "chaos". Training as a solo walk of the tasks; per-mechanic toggles kept to a short list, not 30 |
| Pure random dissident draw (§3) | Streaks feel rigged even when they are fair | A shuffled deal that guarantees everyone gets a human turn before anyone repeats (a fairness bag) |
| No meetings; suspicion is settled by killing; then kicks (§5) | Without a structure, accusations turn into team-kills and kicks | No meetings (decided). Since "outed" never loses, add a light public tag: a shared "spotlight" a player can shine on someone, which the whole party sees |
| Map balanced for 5 to 8; 16 allowed without resizing (§2.3) | Player count needs map scale or rule scale | Scale the task count and the number of humans with the player count, not the map. Document the sweet spot inside 4 to 10 |
