# LOCKDOWN Protocol: a teardown of every task

Research for prime-game ("My Alien Prime Friends"), 2026-10-15. This report is read-only research. Nothing in it is decided.

**Marks.** [V] means I read it at the source myself. [S] means I only saw it in a search-result summary. [P] is player opinion. [A] is my own analysis. Source keys such as (PN 0.1.13) or (G-TASK) link to the list at the end.

**Version caveat.** The detailed task guides date from August to October 2024, which is build 0.1.x. The patch notes after that change task visuals, names and item economy, but they describe no rule rework of any task (see "Changes across patches"). So the 2024 guide mechanics probably still hold at 1.6.1. I could not confirm that in-game.

---

## 1. The frame: round, task deal, task list

**The game.** It is a "first person social deduction game, combining real time action and communication, 1 to 16 players". Most players "work together to collaboratively complete tasks in time", while "a small portion of dissidents" try to stop them [V] (STORE). The Steam API page still says 1 to 16. The map was "balanced for 5-8 players and the size is fine tuned around that", so the developers refused to make it bigger [V] (DEV2). The 16-player limit came only in 0.13.0, behind a "duplicate colors" lobby option, and the team admits it can hardly test that count [V] (PN 0.13.0).

**Win conditions.** Employees win only by finishing every task before the timer runs out. Dissidents win when every employee is dead, or when time runs out with a task still open. Killing all the dissidents does *not* win [V] (NAMU; D-HOW: "dissidents win, when the times runs out with at least one task still being open").

There are **no meetings and no votes**. Anyone can kill anyone, and dissidents only see who their partners are [V] (NAMU). Players also die from overexertion: acting with zero stamina costs health [V] (NAMU; PN 0.1.17).

**Round length.** The developers call it "the current 8 minute round" [V] (DEV2). A 0.1.26 settings screenshot shows `SESSION DURATION 8:00` [V] (SRC-IMG2). In 0.12.1 the maximum setting went from 20 to 60 minutes [V] (PN 0.12.1).

**How tasks are dealt.** There are eight task *types*: Computers, Delivery, Pressure, Analysis, Vents, Alimentation (renamed **Power** in 0.4.0), Pizzushi and Scanner [V] (FANDOM-T; PN 0.4.3). The host lobby tablet (0.1.25 to 0.1.26) shows:
- `TASK COUNT`: how many of the eight types are active this round.
- `DISSIDENT COUNT` (`AUTO` or a number).
- One counter per task type, which sets how many units of that task exist [V] (SRC-IMG1, SRC-IMG2).

The speedrun board writes down the default unit counts: **Computers 3, Delivery 6, Pressure 1, Analysis 6, Vents 3, Alimentations 2, Pizzushi 2, Scanner 8** [V] (SRC-API).

Its "3 Tasks" category ("let's see who gets the luckiest") implies that the types are drawn **at random** when `TASK COUNT` is below 8 [A from V] (SRC-API). The "3 Tasks" screenshot shows 8:00 and task count 3. Its dissident count of 0 shows it is not a pure default screen [V] (SRC-IMG2). My best guess for the Normal preset is 3 random task types in an 8-minute round [A]. I did not see the Normal preset values myself.

The tasks are **shared by the whole crew**: "Employees get tasks and win when all are completed" [V] (D-HOW).

**Presets.** Since 0.1.26 there are four presets, added because "new players are often lost":
- **Training**: learn the tasks.
- **Beginner**: "a simplified version".
- **Normal**: the full experience.
- **Custom**: every setting [V] (PN 0.1.26).

Since 1.2.0, custom presets can be saved [V] (PN 1.2.0).

**Spawns follow the deal.** Task items spawn only when their task is active, and in the amount the counters ask for [V] (G-ITEMS):
- Packages: 6, only with Delivery.
- Gas bottles: 8, only with Pressure.
- Vent filters: equal to the Vents count. Screwdrivers stay at 4 whatever the count.
- Power: each panel that needs charge adds one battery and two fuses that fit it, on top of a baseline of 3 batteries and 9 fuses.
- Containers: +2 for Analysis or Pizzushi, +4 for both.

So the spawner guarantees a solvable round, and sabotage then takes pieces away [A].

**The task list the players see.** The tablet (key Q) shows "your map and task list" [V] (G-TASK). In the task manager, hovering a task makes "white blinking dots" appear in each room, which are the candidate locations. A number next to the task shows how many units are left [V] (TG). The map colours the sectors (red Cameras/Security, yellow Offices, blue Machine, orange Storage, light blue Medical, black and white Pizzushi, green Botanic), and each sector has an icon [V] (G-TASK; FANDOM-MAP).

Hovering Delivery shows every possible drop point [V] (G-RU). The lobby has a big screen with task tutorial videos, and the in-match tablet links them too [V] (PN 0.1.16). Players call these "vague silent film[s]" [P] (D-TUT).

**Scaling with player count.** I found **no automatic scaling** of task units by player count. Every count is a host setting, and dissidents can be `AUTO` [V] (SRC-IMG1). Players say "2 diss + default setting is good for a full lobby" of 8 [P] (D-FULL), and one group reports 2 dissidents in 6 to 8 player games [P] (D-HOST). I did not find the `AUTO` formula.

**How long the work is (solo world records, in-game time).** [V] (SRC-REC)

| Task (default count) | Solo world record |
|---|---|
| Scanner (8) | 41 s |
| Power (2) | 45 s |
| Computers (3) | 67 s |

## 2. Every task

### 2.1 Computers
- **Where:** five office computers in rooms A1 and A2 (in the spawn area), plus one receiving computer in each other sector [V] (G-TASK; NAMU: "a total of five computers").
- **Steps:**
  1. Find an office PC that reads `READY TO SEND FILE TO [X]`.
  2. Press Y (`SEND FILE`). Sending can fail at random (`ERROR OCCURED`), so retry.
  3. Go to the PC in sector X, which shows `WAITING FOR FILE FROM OFFICE [Y]`, and press Y to verify. This can also fail and send you back.
  4. Return to the office PC and confirm.
  5. Repeat for each waiting PC [V] (G-TASK; FANDOM-T).
- **Randomness:** which PCs wait, which sector each one targets, and random send or verify failures [V] (FANDOM-T; NAMU note 2).
- **Players:** solo is possible. A partner at the far PC makes it much faster [V] (G-TASK). Tip: send all files at once and do other tasks on the way [V] (G-TASK).
- **Mistake:** costs only time.
- **Dissident:** every send screen also offers **`N: FAKE`**. Pressing it "will act normally but automatically fail and display the error message as if it failed normally" [V] (FANDOM-T). So a fake looks exactly like bad luck. Players say "If a file verification fails repeatedly, then your partner assisting you may be a Dissident" [P] (G-TASK).
- **Patches:**
  - The earliest note in the feed fixes the task locking "if the Target PC fail the verification" [V] (PN 0.1.6).
  - Sector names were added on the PC screens [V] (PN 0.1.7).
  - 1.0.0 made the PC task "visually … more comprehensive" and easier to translate [V] (PN 1.0.0).

### 2.2 Vents
- **Where:** vents in every major sector and many side rooms. There are none in the plant room, the corridors or the spawn room [V] (G-TASK).
- **Steps:**
  1. Get a screwdriver. There are 4 of them, in Storage and Machine [V] (G-ITEMS).
  2. Search for a vent whose light is **red**. Blue means fine, and **off** means the filter is out: "someone is already working on that vent, or, a Dissident has removed and hidden the filter" [V] (G-TASK).
  3. Unscrew both screws, stow or drop the screwdriver, and take the filter.
  4. Carry it to the filter washer in Machine (blue). Put it in, press the button, and wait until the bar is full.
  5. Carry it back, insert it, and screw it shut. The light turns blue [V] (G-TASK; TG).
- **Randomness:** the broken vents are chosen per round ("3 random locations are selected when the games begins") [V] (G-ITEMS). The tablet dots show where vents *can* be, not which ones are broken [V] (TG: "Visit each dot to find which vents are disabled").
- **A correction to the engineer's memory:** a guide reports that "cleaned vent filters can be placed in other vents, which also need cleaning (you can't open vents which don't need cleaning)" [V] (G-ITEMS). So, by that guide, you need to remember where *an* open vent is, not your exact vent. I could not check this against a patch note. Either way, the "where did I take it from?" problem he described is real. TheGamer's guide advises "Note which rooms have broken vents to avoid confusion" [V] (TG).
- **Players:** solo is fine. A partner can run filters, but a dissident partner can "just steal and hide the filters" [V] (G-TASK).
- **Mistake:** costs only time.
- **Dissident:** pull a filter and hide it, or take the screwdrivers. One guide is all about hiding spots for filters and boxes [V] (G-HIDE). A review complains: "Your team might spend minutes looking for an air filter cartridge that some dissident threw into the corner of a map" [P] (R-FILTER).
- **Patches:** 0.1.19 "Fixed issue with the screws on the vents task" [V]. The screwdriver was rebalanced as a weapon several times [V] (PN 0.1.16, 0.1.25, 0.7.0).

### 2.3 Delivery
- **Where:** packages (6 by default) spawn in Storage. Each sector has delivery boxes [V] (G-ITEMS; G-TASK).
- **Steps:**
  1. Pick up a package and inspect it for its symbol.
  2. Find the box with the same symbol (it has a yellow light).
  3. Press the button, insert the package, and press the button again [V] (G-TASK; NAMU).
- **Constraint:** packages "cannot be stowed", so switching item or opening the map drops them [V] (G-TASK). A package is a heavy weapon (frontal damage 10 health, 70 stamina, 100 knockback) [V] (FANDOM-PKG).
- **Randomness:** which symbol each package carries [A from V].
- **Mistake:** none beyond time.
- **Dissident:** hide packages. Delivery "is one of the easiest for Dissidents to sabotage" [P] (G-TASK). One player wants dissidents able to drop task items into delivery boxes as hiding spots [P] (D-IDEAS).
- **Patches:** packages turn into gifts with the Christmas theme (0.3.0, and from 1.3.0 a setting) [V] (PN 0.3.0, PN 1.3.0).

### 2.4 Scanner
- **Where:** always in Medical, in the scanner room at the back right [V] (G-TASK).
- **Steps:** one player stands on the marked pad. Another reads the monitor and calls out where to look, until every circle has been held and filled [V] (G-TASK; NAMU). The default count of 8 probably means 8 circles [A].
- **Solo:** the monitor beeps faster as your view nears a circle, so you can solo it by ear [V] (G-TASK; NAMU). Several guides and videos teach the solo geometry, for example "the middle of the scanner screen will always be the wall opposite the glass" [V] (G-SCAN; G-UA; YT-SCAN).
- **Randomness:** where the circles sit, per round [A].
- **Players:** "generally need two players", but solo is "hard" [V] (G-TASK).
- **Mistake:** costs only time. The danger is the posture: "Dissidents can easily capitalize on backstabbing you while you are looking around" [P] (G-TASK). One video is titled "Pile of dead bodies in scanner?!" (YT-SCAN).
- **Patches:** none found. Speedruns require the scanner to be "clearly audible" [V] (SRC-API).

### 2.5 Pressure (the hard one)
- **Where:** two machines on the right of the Machine room entrance. Each has 4 canister slots, an up button, a down button, a gauge and a status light [V] (G-PRES; G-TASK).
- **Items:** always 8 gas bottles: **3 yellow (1), 3 red (2), 2 blue (3)**. Six are on the Storage shelves and two start in the valves [V] (G-PRES; G-ITEMS).
- **Hidden rule:** "every game the slots in the machine are assigned a random number value 1-3." A canister must be greater than or equal to its slot's value. Half power tests each slot at half its value, full power at its full value [V] (G-PRES). The guide's own reading: "the color and number match the maximum pressure the gas canister can handle" [V] (G-TASK).
- **Steps, as players solved it:**
  1. Carry all 8 bottles to Machine.
  2. Load 1-1-1-2 (all yellow and one red).
  3. Press up once for half power. A yellow that pops out marks a slot that needs blue.
  4. Go to full power. A yellow that pops needs red, a red that pops needs blue.
  5. Press down before swapping, because canisters cannot be removed while the machine runs.
  6. Repeat on the second machine with what is left.

  Status lights: red means incomplete, green means the pressure holds, blue means done [V] (G-PRES; G-RU; D-SO-BAD). Because a 2 also fits a slot that needs 1, a wrong guess on the first machine can make the second unsolvable, so you have to "swap a red from the first machine with a yellow from the second" [V] (G-PRES; D-PRES).
- **Mistake:** a wrong canister makes the machine **explode and fire the canister out**. Standing in its path is lethal: "do not stand in front of the engine, otherwise the flying canister will kill you" [V] (G-RU, translated). Another guide: "stay against the wall away from the canisters to ensure you don't die" [V] (D-TUT). A review simply says "Don't stand in front of pressure" [P] (R-PRES).

  Since 0.1.13, "There is now a cooldown when the pressure machine explode, before you can interact with it again. This encourage using the blinking lights to finish the task faster" [V] (PN 0.1.13). Red blinking lights warn of the failing slot before the blast, so a partner can call it out [V] (G-TASK). Players also call the cooldown a window to be attacked [P] (D-SO-BAD).
- **"A mistake killed everyone":** not confirmed. Every source describes harm to whoever is in the canister's path. None says the whole lobby dies [V by absence] (G-TASK; G-RU; NAMU: "If you are exposed to an explosion at this time, you will receive damage"). The engineer's group may all have stood in front of the machine [A]. The truly lobby-wide killer in the game is the C4 with a global-range detonator [V] (G-ITEMS).
- **Dissident:** many sabotages, all through actions [V] (G-TASK):
  - Hide one bottle. There are exactly 8, so one missing bottle blocks the task.
  - Turn a machine off and pull its bottles, which "can force you to need to completely start over".
  - Swap a bottle so that the next power-up blows up on a teammate.

  The guide even advises the reverse: "do not be afraid to weaponize the machine". 0.5.0 "Removed collision inside the pressure machines", so items can be hidden inside them [V] (PN 0.5.0).
- **Players:** solo is possible. Experienced players argue that solo is *safer* ("pop them every times… and do it solo… than having someone pop in your face") [P] (D-SO-BAD). A two-person version uses the blinking lights.
- **What players say:**
  - "no one knows how to do pressure they just blow that up until it works" [P] (D-SO-BAD).
  - "I didnt even know i could remove pressure tanks" [P] (D-TUT).
  - A 6-player group "agreed to disable Pressure in future games": "it's very easy to block the employees by hiding just one … of the canisters" [P] (D-FAIL).
  - Guides and videos: "Pressure Task Explanation" ("Many people (me included) seemed to be having troubles with this task") (G-PRES); "You're doing Pressure WRONG! … ADVANCED tutorial" (YT-PRES); "Annie Under Pressure" (YT-ANNIE).
- **Patches:** 0.1.13 cooldown; 0.5.0 hiding inside the machines; 1.0.0 "Slightly changed the Power and Pressure panels to remove unnecessary text" [V].

### 2.6 Power (formerly Alimentation)
- **Where:** panels in every sector, often in secluded spots. Battery chargers are in Machine: two slow ones and a faster one with a yellow mark [V] (G-TASK; G-RU; G-UA).
- **Panel:** a battery slot, two fuse slots, a colour at each end of the fuse row, a charge-needed bar, and a battery-charge bar. Red light means it needs work, blue means done [V] (G-TASK).
- **Fuses:** two-coloured, from yellow, red and blue. The two fuses must form a colour chain from the left end colour to the right. Example: for blue to red, `Blue/Yellow → Yellow/Red`, `Blue/Red → Red/Red` or `Blue/Blue → Blue/Red` [V] (G-TASK). The Russian guide calls it arranging "by gradient" [V] (G-RU).
- **Steps:**
  1. Find a red panel and read its two end colours.
  2. Check or replace the battery. An empty battery needs 15 s on a charger [V] (FANDOM-BAT).
  3. Fetch the matching fuses from Storage or Machine.
  4. Insert them and wait for the bar to fill. The light turns blue.
  5. The battery and fuses can then be reused elsewhere [V] (G-TASK).

  Panels sometimes start with a battery or fuses already inserted, possibly in the wrong order [V] (G-ITEMS).
- **Randomness:** which panels are red, their end colours, which fuses spawn and which fuses start in the panels [V] (G-ITEMS).
- **Effort:** the guide calls it "the longest task … and the easiest of all tasks for Dissidents to sabotage", because it "sometimes requires visiting all rooms" [P] (G-TASK). Yet the solo world record is only 45 s [V] (SRC-REC). That is a good example of a task that is easy for experts and slow for beginners [A].
- **Mistake:** costs only time.
- **Dissident:** steal batteries ("always needed"), hide fuses, or hide fuses *inside completed panels* where nobody looks [V] (G-TASK).
- **Coupling:** the same parts feed other systems [V]:
  - Alarms need a white fuse (PN 0.5.0).
  - The defibrillator charges from two batteries at once (PN 0.7.0).
  - A locked supply room holds fast chargers and "random extra supply for certain tasks" (PN 0.8.0).
- **Patches:** 0.4.0 renamed it to Power [V] (PN 0.4.3). 1.1.0 "Increased battery recharge speed… the recharge time felt a bit out of place and broke the flow of the game" [V]. 1.2.0 added a charge-speed multiplier from 50 to 200% [V].

### 2.7 Pizzushi
- **Where:** the restaurant kitchen has 3 rice dispensers, an assembly machine and a fish vending machine. Rice bags are in Storage or the pantry, and plants are in the Botanic plant room [V] (G-TASK).
- **Order:** a placemat with a yellow light on a table shows rice, fish and plant, for example White Rice + Salmon + WX2. The 2 default recipes come from: 3 rices (white, brown, black), 4 fish (salmon, tuna, shrimp, cod) and 5 plants (WX2 white, RU2 red, BO4 blue, Y8Z yellow, G3M green; read the first letter as the colour) [V] (G-TASK; G-UA; D-TUT).
- **Steps:**
  1. Load the rice bags into the dispensers. You only learn which rice a bag holds after loading it [V] (G-UA).
  2. Fill a clean container with the right rice.
  3. Dispense the fish.
  4. Fill a second container with the plant.
  5. Slot all three into the assembly machine and press its buttons left to right, waiting for each process.
  6. Carry the boxed pizza to its placemat [V] (G-TASK).
  7. Wash dirty containers in Botanic [V] (G-TASK).
- **Mistake:** a wrong ingredient gives a wrong pizza. The recipe light turns red [S] (search summary of a Steam post). The fix is to "just finish out your mistake… throw it to the side, wash the canisters and start again" [P] (D-FAIL).
- **Dissident:** hide rice, containers or fish, or deliberately make a wrong order. "Watch employees, they may deliberately make orders not per the recipe" [V] (G-UA, translated). A video is titled "I sabotaged Pizzushi 5 times in a row and no one noticed" (YT-PIZ).
- **Players:** solo is possible. Pairs are faster [P] (G-TASK).
- **Patches:** 0.2.0 made restaurant tables unwalkable, fixing "an exploit where players could intentionally die on the table to hide the recipe" [V]. 1.0.0 overhauled the visuals (a model per topping, a new menu, the fish dispenser) and fixed "dummy samples from rice containers in the sample machine" [V]. 1.2.0 made rice bags spawn even without the task, "So you can now make Pizzushi with friends no matter the available tasks" [V].

### 2.8 Analysis
- **Where:** the Botanic analysis room, with three machines left to right: analysis, extraction and mixing, plus a monitor. Next door are the plant room and a container-washing room [V] (G-TASK; FANDOM-T).
- **Steps:**
  1. Read the monitor. Each block (6 by default) asks for 1 plant, or 2 plants to mix (for example WX2+RU2).
  2. Fill a container at the right plant.
  3. Extract it into a sample. The container comes out dirty.
  4. Mix two samples if the block asks for it.
  5. Analyse the sample.
  6. Wash the containers and repeat [V] (G-TASK; FANDOM-T).
- **Bottleneck:** containers. The guide recommends holding 3. The same containers feed Pizzushi, and since 0.11.0 the gun reload station too [V] (G-TASK; FANDOM-ARM).
- **Mistake:** costs only time.
- **Dissident:** steal containers. The room is isolated, so dissidents "claim kills more easily" there [P] (G-TASK). A review: "10/10 would fail the analysis again" [P] (R-ANA).
- **Patches:** early netcode fixes for the sample slots [V] (PN 0.1.7, 0.1.9, 0.1.11).

### 2.9 Task-like systems that are not tasks
- **Weapon crates (0.2.0 to 0.3.0):** a 4-digit code with no repeated digits. Each guess colours the digits: grey means absent, yellow means present but misplaced, blue means correct. That is a Mastermind puzzle [V] (G-CRATE). You get 7 tries (down from 9). A failed code locks the crate "for a random time", and crates unlock at random during the round [V] (PN 0.2.2). Foam keeps the shape of the gun that was taken, so everyone can see what came out [V] (PN 0.2.0).
- **Alarms (0.5.0):** one per sector. Each needs a white fuse, and not every alarm starts with one. Pulling the fuse "will permanently disable the alarm" [V]. Since 0.8.0 an alarm turns itself off after 10 s [V]. Players call them "entirely useless noise machines" and want an alarm to freeze the timer and call a vote [P] (D-GENRE).
- **Defibrillator (0.7.0):** it hangs on a wall in Medical and must be charged by filling **two battery slots at once**. One charge either "resurrect[s] a dead player, or deal[s] heavy damage on a living one" [V] (PN 0.7.0). 0.9.0 added 3 more in a locked Emergency Room, and 0.12.0 made those spawn pre-charged [V]. Players got a story out of it: "I revived them with the defibrillator and immediately killed them again with it" [P] (R-DEFIB). They also complained: "a weapon (defib) that can 1 shot anyone infinite amounts of times" [P] (D-GENRE).
- **Access card rooms (0.8.0 to 0.11.0):** one card per match chooses which of the locked rooms to open: the supply room, the research corridor, the emergency room or the armory ("only one of the 3 rooms can be opened per match, so make sure you choose wisely") [V] (PN 0.9.0). In 0.11.0 a second card can spawn in the camera room, and in 1.2.0 the default became 2 cards [V].
- **Gun reload station (0.11.0):** reload a weapon by inserting the matching *plant sample* (shorty RU2, pistol BO4, revolver Y8Z), so guns now depend on the Analysis economy [V] (PN 0.11.0; FANDOM-ARM). Since 0.12.0 each refill gives half the ammo [V].

## 5. What makes it work [A, backed by the facts above]

1. **Every task is physical.** Bottles, filters, fuses, packages and containers are objects in the world that anyone can carry, hide, throw or swing. Sabotage therefore needs no special powers: a dissident does what an employee could do, just in the wrong place. This matches our "roles are mechanically identical" rule (m8-match §2).
2. **Shared parts tie the systems together.** Batteries feed Power, the defibrillator and alarm logic. Fuses feed Power and alarms. Containers and plants feed Analysis, Pizzushi and gun reloads. One stolen container hurts three systems, and someone hoarding them stands out.
3. **The tasks stretch the crew across the map.** Vents, Power and Delivery need every sector, so people split up and pass each other. Exposure is a cost of doing tasks.
4. **Each round is shuffled.** Which task types are active, which vents and panels are broken, the slot thresholds, the recipes and the file targets all change. One map stays fresh for a while.
5. **Puzzles you can learn, with a dramatic failure.** Pressure and the crate code are small deduction games with a method players can teach each other. Pressure's explosion turns a mistake into a story and a weapon.
6. **Fakes that look like bad luck.** The `N: FAKE` button and the random send failures make sabotage indistinguishable from chance. Distrust grows with no lying needed.
7. **You can solo by ear.** Scanner can be done alone by listening, and Pressure alone by watching for pops. Two-person tasks never fully strand a lone player.
8. **States you can read at a glance.** Red, blue and off lights on vents and panels. The "off" state is deliberately ambiguous: someone is working on it, or someone sabotaged it.
9. **The timer and having no meetings keep the pace up.** Eight minutes, no voting, and dissidents win on time.

## 6. What players complain about

- **Unclear tasks and bad tutorials:** "tasks are needlessly obtuse and convoluted" (R-OBTUSE); the tutorial "teaches you 0 information you need to know to do tasks" (R-TUT); "vague silent film" tutorials (D-TUT) [P].
- **Hiding one item hard-blocks a task.** One canister or one food item blocks it, so groups disable Pressure (D-FAIL) or spend minutes hunting a filter (R-FILTER) [P].
- **Losing on time after the dissidents are dead:** "feels very disappointing when the 'imposters' are dead and you still lose to tasks" (R-TIMER) [P].
- **Mastery kills the dissident side:** "you'll very quickly … get so good at the tasks on the single map … that the traitor has no chance" (R-MASTER) [P]. Review aggregates also report that dissidents need "more distinct sabotage mechanics" (VAPOR) [P].
- **Weapons crowd out the tasks:** "someone gets guns and everyone dies" (R-GUNS); "once a Dissident has a weapon with enough ammo it's just over" (D-GENRE) [P]. The developers' own fixes were crates (0.2.0), then nerfs (0.14.4 and 0.15.0) "to leave more space for the social aspect" [V].
- **Too few tasks, one map:** "needs some more tasks though" (R-MORE); aggregates put "lack of new maps, game modes, and varied tasks" high on the list (VAPOR) [P].
- **Role streaks:** repeat dissidents and a suspected host bias (D-SAME; D-HOST) [P].

## 7. Mechanic → lesson → an original direction for our alien party

Every entry in the third column is **a proposal for the engineer, not decided**. None reuses LOCKDOWN Protocol's names or designs. Each one has to fit the content area's rules: the engineer's word, no invented numbers that become rules, Delivery "by meaning, not colour" (GDD §8), and the pillars "never boring", "macro over micro" and "open knowledge" (GDD §1).

| Mechanic in LOCKDOWN Protocol | What it teaches us | Original direction for our alien party (proposal) |
|---|---|---|
| Vents: random broken units, remove, process at a far station, return; the light shows state | A search, carry and return loop makes people roam and adds a memory load that creates funny fails | **Proposal:** a few random party fixtures across the House are "out of vibe". You take out their core, recharge it at the ship's tech in the garage, and bring it back. The empty socket plays a sad little jingle you hear only nearby, so the forgetful find their way back **by ear** (it uses proximity audio; the open-knowledge pillar allows it) |
| Pressure: hidden per-slot thresholds, half and full tests, an explosion that ejects the part, a cooldown | A learnable deduction puzzle with a spectacular, dangerous failure gives the stories players retell. Its weakness: exactly 8 parts, so one hidden part hard-blocks it | **Proposal:** the aliens' "party punch reactor": load drinks of unknown fizz into a machine and test it at low, then full power. A wrong drink blows the lid and **knocks down everyone in the room**, which with our respawns costs time, not the match (m8-match §3). Because it is loud, it also **raises the neighbours' anger** (m8-match §5). Keep one or two spare drinks so hiding one never hard-blocks it |
| Power: chain two-colour fuses between two end colours; a charged battery | A matching puzzle read at the station; the parts are shared with other systems | **Proposal:** chain by **meaning**, not colour, as the engineer chose for Delivery: the stereo needs two cartridges that lead from the room's current mood to the requested one (for example "chill" to "wild" through "warm-up"). The batteries come from the same charger that other gadgets need |
| Computers: send, verify, confirm across rooms; random failure; a FAKE button | A sabotage indistinguishable from bad luck spreads distrust with no lying | **Proposal:** an alien broadcasts the party to the mothership from the attic; a receiver in the basement confirms. The signal drops at random, and anyone can press "static" on purpose. It fits "actions, not lying": a player who faked it never has to say anything |
| Scanner: one player does, one guides; solo by ear | A two-role voice task that never strands a lone player | **Proposal:** dance-floor calibration: a "DJ" at the screen shouts directions to a dancer on the pad; solo, you follow the beat's tempo. Check the overlap with Photo (sitter and shooter, GDD §8) |
| Pizzushi and Analysis: combinations from several zones, staged processing with waits, a shared container shortage | Waits invite doing two things at once; a scarce shared consumable makes contention and readable hoarding | **Proposal:** party cups are the shared consumable for the punch, the cocktail station and the cake (m8-match §5 "bake a cake"). Washing them is an optional side job. Cooking (the burger) stays the single combination task, so they don't overlap |
| Delivery: carried by hand, can't be stowed, heavy as a weapon | Carrying makes you visible and vulnerable; a choice between work and defence | Already ours (Delivery by meaning). **Proposal:** bulky gifts take both hands, so the carrier can't hold a weapon. Escorting a carrier becomes a role |
| Weapon crate: Mastermind code, 7 tries, a random lockout; the foam shows what was taken | A deduction gate on power items, plus a public trace of who took what | **Proposal:** an alien piñata with a combination lock. A failed code locks it for a while. The empty piñata keeps the outline of what fell out. It fits "open knowledge" |
| Defibrillator: charged by two parts at once; revives or harms | A dual-use tool that drains the task economy and makes stories | **Proposal (for M8.3 mutations):** a "re-materializer" that calls a dead alien back early at its spot, charged by two parts put in at once, so two players must cooperate. It never works as damage, which suits the comic-violence tone |
| Alarm: fuse-fed, permanently disabled by pulling the fuse; players found it useless | A global signal must *do* something, or it is noise | **Proposal:** a party horn that summons everyone to a spot and briefly freezes the timer. A human can unplug it for good, which is itself mischief |
| One access card picks one of several locked rooms | A strategic choice per match from a small menu | **Proposal:** a single VIP wristband opens one of the rooftop, the pool or the cellar per match, each with a different bonus |
| `TASK COUNT` draws N of 8 types; presets from Training to Custom; spawns match the deal | Rotation from a pool, plus a guaranteed solvable spawn, keeps one map fresh; presets help newcomers | **Proposal:** a larger pool of party "chores" with a random subset per match, shown on a fridge whiteboard. A "Training" preset per task; spawns guarantee what is needed plus a small spare |
| Lights: red, blue, off; "off" is ambiguous | Readable at a glance, with built-in doubt | **Proposal:** every station shows three states (fine, needs work, empty or in progress); "empty" stays ambiguous on purpose |
