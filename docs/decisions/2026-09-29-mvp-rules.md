# MVP rules

- **Status:** Provisional: decided by the engineer; the designer may change it
- **Date:** 2026-09-29
- **Deciders:** the engineer, in chat with the M2 manager session on 2026-09-29, including the answers Q1 to Q6;
  recorded from #31. Extended the same day with the engineer's answers to #32 (Q7, Q8 and the gaps found there).
  The designer reviews it in #38. Corrected by the engineer on 2026-09-30 in #46: ghosts do not fly, players
  push each other, and being pushed costs no stamina (below).

## Context
M2 starts the real game, and the rules it builds need a source every session can read: the chat in which the engineer
decided them is not visible to other sessions. Lockdown Protocol is the main reference; the look is low-poly and first
person, roughly like How to Fish. The goal is an extensible framework on that base, not a copy: roles, task types, win
conditions and game modes are modular, so a new mechanic needs no rewrite of the core. Visuals stay minimal
(primitives, simple low-poly models); the focus is on mechanics. Everything key is configurable.

The phases these rules run in: [game modes define the phases](2026-09-29-game-modes-define-the-phases.md). Hosting,
disconnects and the network: [listen server and message layer](2026-09-29-listen-server-and-message-layer.md).

## Decision

### Roles
- Two roles: **Crew** and **Dissident**. Both receive tasks and can do them; a task a dissident completes counts
  toward the shared progress like anyone's.
- The number of dissidents is a lobby setting from 0 to N−1 (N = players). Dissidents know each other.

### Win conditions
- The **crew** wins when all tasks are done before the match timer ends. This is the crew's only way to win.
- The **dissidents** win when the timer ends with tasks left, or when every crew member is dead.
- Killing a dissident gives the crew nothing by itself.
- With 0 dissidents, the timer ending is still a dissident win: the crew loses.

### Tasks
- A task has 1 to 10 subtasks (a lobby setting). Tasks are dealt to players; the task count is per player.
- The only MVP task type is **Delivery**:
  - At match start, packages are scattered at random over the map's package spawn points.
  - Each package has a destination department: in the MVP, a circle on the map. The circles are also placed at
    random at match start, over the map's circle spawn points. A package and its circle share a colour, so a player
    sees which circle a package belongs to.
  - One package is one subtask.
  - A subtask is done when the package **rests inside its destination circle, however it got there** (put down,
    swapped, dropped by a dying or leaving player; later thrown). Standing on the circle while holding it is not
    enough. (The engineer's correction in #32, 2026-09-29; it replaces "put down so that it rests on its circle".)
  - A delivered package counts for good and is no longer interactive: nobody can pick it up (Q3). Its circle is
    shown as done.
  - Any living player may carry and deliver any package; the delivery counts toward that package's task. So an idle
    or dead player cannot block the crew.
  - The package count is derived: players × tasks per player × subtasks per task. It is shown in the lobby, not a
    separate setting.
- New task types must be addable later, for example "stand in a zone for N seconds" (#36).

### Items
- One hand slot. Any item can be picked up and put down. In the MVP a key places the held item on the ground in front
  of the player; physics throwing comes later (#37).
- Packages and weapons are both items: holding a weapon means not holding a package.

### Health and stamina
- Every player has health and stamina. At 0 health the player dies.
- Sprint (Shift) speeds movement up and spends stamina; a jump spends stamina; a weapon hit spends stamina.
- Stamina regenerates while it is not being spent. Health does not regenerate in the MVP (Q4).
- Each player sees only their own health and stamina (Q5).

### Collisions
Living players never pass through each other, but a body cannot block a passage (the engineer's decision of
2026-09-30 in #46, read back and confirmed):
- Walking into a player pushes them: you both move on, slower than your own speed. You slow down, and they are pushed
  at that reduced speed (the push speed factor, a placeholder of about half).
- Head-on, the pushes along the line of contact cancel, so nobody advances. The bodies are round, so any sideways
  motion or a small angle makes them slide off each other and part; even a perfectly straight contact gets a tiny
  sideways drift, so the two never freeze like two walls. It should feel lively, not mechanical.
- Ghosts push nobody and are pushed by nobody.
- Being pushed costs no stamina: sprint spends stamina only on the player's own movement, so a pushed player holding
  Shift pays nothing for the push (the engineer's decision of 2026-09-30, #46).
- The network model does not change: each client moves only its own player. The pushed player's client moves its
  player from the pusher's interpolated motion (about 0.1 s late), and the host tolerates overlap and does not
  correct it (ARCHITECTURE §7.1 "Pushing apart").

### Weapon
- The base weapon, a **knife**, is an item taken into the hand. Knives are scattered at random at match start, like
  packages; their count is a lobby setting (Q1).
- Using a knife hits: every living player inside the hit zone in front takes damage, and the hit spends stamina (Q2).
- The hit zone (angle and reach) is set per weapon. The knife's zone is narrow and short, so that it almost never
  touches two players, because players push each other apart. Later weapons may have a wider angle and a longer
  reach.
- Anyone can hit anyone.

### Death and ghosts
- The body stays on the map, and the dead player becomes a **ghost**.
- Ghosts cannot interact with the world at all: they only watch, talk (with the dead) and move. They cannot pick up,
  carry or use items.
- Ghosts move like the living: the same collider (Q6), gravity, floor, steps, slopes (no steeper ones than the
  living climb), jump and sprint with Shift. The engineer's intent: a ghost reaches no place a living player cannot
  (one open exception, the jump distance, is the next bullet). Their walk and sprint speeds are the living's times a
  ghost speed factor of **1.3** (the engineer's decision), their jump height is the living's, and their stamina is
  unlimited. They do not pass through walls, and they do not collide with the living or with other ghosts: an
  invisible blocker would give them away. There is no free flight. (The engineer's correction of 2026-09-30 in #46: "ghosts fly" meant
  the look, a floating head with no legs, not the movement.)
- A side effect, not decided yet: with the same jump and 30% more speed, a ghost jumps 30% farther, so it could cross
  a gap a living player cannot. Recommended for the MVP: accept it and keep such gaps out of the greybox map; the
  alternative is to cap a ghost's horizontal speed in the air at the living's.
- Other ghosts see a ghost as a small sphere, like a head, instead of a body. The living neither see nor hear ghosts.
- Ghosts do not see other players' roles.
- Resurrection comes later (#34).

### Voice
- Proximity voice between the living. The lobby uses proximity voice too.
- The dead are a separate group, but they hear by distance too, like the living: a ghost hears the living and other
  ghosts only within a radius of the ghost, so not in another room. Walls (occlusion, M5) will apply to ghosts as to
  the living. (The engineer's correction in #32, 2026-09-29; it replaces "the dead hear each other anywhere on the
  map".)
- The living never hear the dead.

### Meetings and voting
None in the base mode. The architecture must allow a meetings mode later, as a separate game mode (#35).

### Match loop
1. Players join the host and arrive in the lobby scene, where they can walk and talk and nothing else.
2. Each player presses Ready.
3. When all are ready, a 5-second countdown starts; anyone un-readying, joining or leaving cancels it.
4. After the countdown everyone loads the game scene; roles are dealt and packages scattered.
5. At the end, an end screen says only which side won: "The crew won" or "The dissidents won". It names no players
   and shows no roles; players discuss their roles in the lobby afterwards. The host's button returns everyone to the lobby for the next match. While
   the end screen is up the game is frozen: the screen is black apart from the result, and nobody hears anybody.
   (The engineer's correction in #32, 2026-09-29; it replaces "shows the winner and every role".)

Voice exists only in the lobby (its countdown included) and in the round; there is none while loading or on the end
screen (#32).

### Match settings
Changed by the host in a lobby UI between matches: match duration (minutes); tasks per player; subtasks per task
(1 to 10); dissidents (0 to N−1); knives. The derived package count is shown next to them.

### HUD
Health, stamina, and the name of the held item; a package also shows its destination.

## The engineer's answers to #32 (2026-09-29)
Asked in #32's session as one batched question; the engineer answered "A" (the recommended option) to each, except
where noted.
- **Q7 At 0 stamina:** walking always works. Sprint, jump and a knife hit need at least their full cost, so they are
  unavailable until stamina regenerates that far. For sprint the full cost is one second of sprinting; once started,
  a sprint lasts until stamina reaches 0 or Shift is released.
- **Q8 Settings defaults:** placeholders marked "not a decision", in the table above.
- **Leaving or hanging during Loading** (roles are not dealt yet): that peer is dropped and the round starts with
  the peers that confirmed. A peer that has not confirmed within the loading timeout counts as leaving.
- **What each player learns in the base mode:** everything physical is public to every player of the match: where
  the living are, bodies and whose they are, items on the ground and in hands, a package's destination, the shared
  task progress, knife swings. Hidden: roles (except that dissidents know each other), a player's own tasks, their
  health and stamina, damage (only the victim learns it; the attacker gets no hit confirmation), and ghosts (only the
  dead see them). The interface does not announce deaths. Positions are not hidden behind walls: that would protect
  against cheating clients, which nobody asked for.
- **A client leaving mid-round:** the avatar disappears, everyone sees "X left", and no body stays. The held item
  drops where the player stood; their packages stay in the game.
- **Voice on the end screen** (the engineer's own answer): none; the game is frozen and nobody hears anybody (loop
  step 5).
- **Small rules:**
  - Picking an item up with a full hand swaps them (the engineer's own answer): the held item is put down where the
    picked-up item lay.
  - A weapon has a minimum time between hits (the knife's is in the table).
  - The host changes the settings only in the lobby phase; a change un-readies nobody; during the countdown the
    settings are locked.
  - Ghosts collide with walls only: not with the living, and not with other ghosts.
  - Several things in one tick: the tick's commands apply in the order the host received them, and the first win
    condition met ends the round. The timer is checked after the tick's commands, so a delivery in the last tick
    counts.
  - A dying player's held item drops at the body, and the ghost appears there.
  - New placeholder numbers: in the table above.

## The engineer's answers Q1 to Q6 (2026-09-29)
- **Q1 Where knives come from:** scattered at random at match start like packages; the count is a lobby setting.
- **Q2 What a hit damages:** every living player in the hit zone. The zone is set per weapon (angle and reach), and
  the knife's is so narrow and short that it almost never touches two players, because players' colliders push each
  other apart. Later weapons may have a wider angle and a longer reach.
- **Q3 A delivered package:** it counts for good and can no longer be picked up.
- **Q4 Regeneration:** stamina regenerates while not being spent; health does not regenerate in the MVP.
- **Q5 Other players' health:** each player sees only their own.
- **Q6 How ghosts move:** neither option as asked. Ghosts fly, faster than the living, but do not pass through walls;
  for now their collider is the same as a living player's. Other ghosts see them as a small sphere, like a head. They
  do not collide with the living. (Corrected on 2026-09-30, see below: "fly" was the look, not the movement.)

Corrected by the engineer the same day: ghosts only watch, talk and move (faster than the living), and with 0
dissidents the timer ending is a dissident win.

Corrected by the engineer on 2026-09-30 in #46: in Q6 "fly" was the look (a floating head with no legs), not the
movement. Ghosts move like the living (no flight, no fly-down key), at 1.3 times the living's walk and sprint speeds,
with the living's jump height and unlimited stamina (see "Death and ghosts").
